Outbound gets better with a filter on

Kaan UnOctober 8, 202621 reactions · 5 commentsView on LinkedIn ->

  • Outbound
  • Ideal customer profile
  • Intent signals
  • Clay

Breakout product builders subtract far more than they add. Apple, Dyson and SpaceX all work that way, and so does good intent outbound.

A wide net makes every number worse

When the net is too wide, click rates drop. That much makes sense: you cannot sell B2B software to a consumer brand, however good the email is.

Your sequences should keep landing on people who look like your ideal customers. So when the copy reads well and the replies are still thin, look at the sheet before you touch the words.

Fix the list in this order

  1. Start with your last five accounts that paid. Write down the industry, the headcount, who signed, and what they bought. That note is your ICP, and it sits next to the sheet. A row has to match it before it stays.
  2. Pull that profile from Apollo.io, then from a second source, and merge both in Clay. One database misses accounts that fit and keeps accounts that do not.
  3. Mark who is in market before anyone writes a word. An account is in market when it is hiring the person who buys, already using a tool in the category, or sitting on your pricing page. No signal this month, and that account waits.
  4. Remove the rows that fail the note. A B2B product and a B2C company do not belong on the same list. Take off the wrong headcount, and any account with no one who looks like the person who signed last time.
  5. Send the sequence to the accounts that remain. Write the opener, the follow up and the offer from what those five customers bought.

Why the last five paying customers

A persona document describes who you hope will buy. Your last five invoices describe who did. They carry the details a workshop never surfaces: the exact title on the signature, the headcount band, the product that closed the deal. Start there and the ICP is a fact you can check each row against.

Two sources beat one

Every database has holes. Pull the same profile from two providers and merge them, and the accounts one source missed show up from the other. The merge also exposes rows that only one source believes in, which are often the ones worth a second look before they reach a sequence.

What changes after the cut

The same three emails now go to a shorter list, and that list looks like the accounts that already pay you. So you write those emails for a buyer you already know how to sell to.

The filter is also where a pipeline saves most of its money. Read why it belongs before enrichment.

Look at the sheet first. The copy can wait a day.
